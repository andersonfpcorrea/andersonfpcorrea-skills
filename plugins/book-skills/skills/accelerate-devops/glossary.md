# Glossary — Accelerate: The Science of Lean Software and DevOps

**Batch size** — The amount of work moving through a system at once; smaller batches reduce cycle times, accelerate feedback, and lower risk. Deployment frequency is its proxy in software (Ch 2).

**Burnout** — Physical, mental, or emotional exhaustion caused by overwork or stress, manifesting as cynicism, ineffectiveness, and work spilling into personal life. Caused by organizational factors, not individual weakness (Ch 9).

**Capabilities model** — A framework measuring specific, improvable behaviors linked to outcomes; contrasted with maturity model, which prescribes static levels (Ch 1).

**Catchball** — ING's pattern of escalating problems upward and relaying solutions downward through stand-ups at each organizational level; implements Hoshin Kanri (Ch 16).

**Change Advisory Board (CAB)** — External body reviewing production changes. Negatively correlated with tempo and stability; no correlation with change failure rate (Ch 7).

**Change failure rate** — Percentage of deployments causing degraded service or requiring remediation (hotfix, rollback, patch). One of the four DORA metrics; measures stability (Ch 2).

**Cluster analysis** — Statistical technique grouping responses so that members of the same group are more similar to each other than to other groups; used to derive high/medium/low performance tiers (Ch 2, Ch 12).

**Configuration drift** — Divergence between environment configurations over time due to manual changes; automated configuration management prevents it (Ch 9).

**Construct (latent construct)** — A statistical measurement of something that cannot be directly observed, composed of multiple survey items validated as reliably measuring the same underlying concept (Ch 13).

**Continuous delivery (CD)** — A development practice where software is in a deployable state throughout its lifecycle; the team prioritizes deployability over new features; fast feedback on deployability is available to all (Ch 4, Appendix A).

**Continuous integration (CI)** — Practice of regularly integrating code to a shared trunk, each check-in triggering automated tests; creates canonical builds. Short-lived branches (<1 day) are key (Ch 4).

**Conway's Law** — "Organizations which design systems are constrained to produce designs which are copies of the communication structures of these organizations" (Melvin Conway, 1968) (Ch 5).

**Deployment frequency** — How often code is deployed to production or to end users. Proxy for batch size; one of the four DORA metrics (Ch 2).

**Deployment pain** — Fear and anxiety engineers feel when deploying to production; a measurable indicator of delivery process quality and predictor of delivery/organizational performance (Ch 9).

**DORA metrics** — The four key metrics: deployment frequency, lead time for changes, MTTR (mean time to restore), and change failure rate (Ch 2).

**eNPS (Employee Net Promoter Score)** — Measure of employee loyalty: % promoters (9–10) minus % detractors (0–6) on the question "Would you recommend this org/team as a place to work?" (Ch 10).

**Failure demand** — Work caused by failing to do the right thing the first time; unplanned/rework; reduced by continuous delivery (Ch 4).

**Generative culture** — Performance-oriented Westrum culture type: high cooperation, messengers trained, risks shared, bridging encouraged, failure leads to inquiry, novelty implemented (Ch 3).

**Hoshin Kanri** — Lean strategy deployment: aligning front-line team learning with enterprise strategy priorities through a vertical/horizontal communication rhythm (Ch 16).

**Identity** — The degree to which employees identify with their organization's values and goals; predicts generative culture and organizational performance (Ch 10).

**Inferential predictive analysis** — Theory-driven hypothesis testing using survey data; goes beyond correlation to establish prediction relationships. The primary analysis type in this book (Ch 12).

**Inverse Conway Maneuver** — Deliberately evolving team and org structure to achieve a desired architecture — the reverse of Conway's Law applied proactively (Ch 5).

**Lead time for changes** — Time from code commit to code running successfully in production. One of the four DORA metrics; measures tempo (Ch 2).

**Lean management** — Toyota-derived management philosophy; applied here as WIP limits, visual displays, and monitoring-driven decisions (Ch 7).

**Loosely coupled architecture** — System design enabling teams to test, deploy, and change their components independently without coordination dependencies (Ch 5).

**Maturity model** — Framework defining static levels of capability; prescribes lock-step progression; treats organizations as "done" at a certain level (Ch 1).

**Mean time to restore (MTTR)** — Time to restore service after an incident. One of the four DORA metrics; measures stability (Ch 2).

**Obeya** — Japanese for "large room"; a visual management space where strategic objectives, progress, and problems are made continuously visible (Ch 16).

**Pathological culture** — Power-oriented Westrum culture type: low cooperation, messengers shot, responsibilities shirked, failure leads to scapegoating (Ch 3).

**PDCA** — Plan-Do-Check-Act; the foundational continuous improvement cycle applied at all levels of the ING organization (Ch 16).

**Shift left** — Moving a concern (typically security or testing) earlier in the delivery lifecycle rather than addressing it as a downstream gate (Ch 6).

**Snowball sampling** — Research method where initial participants refer others; appropriate when the population cannot be enumerated (Ch 15).

**Squad** — ING term for a self-steering cross-functional team with a distinct customer mission; sized by the Two Pizza Rule (Ch 16).

**Transformational leadership** — Leadership style inspiring followers to identify with organizational objectives through vision, inspirational communication, intellectual stimulation, supportive leadership, and personal recognition (Ch 11).

**Trunk-based development** — Practice of developing against the main branch with <3 active branches and branch lifetimes <1 day; predictor of high software delivery performance (Ch 4).

**Virtuous cycle** — Reciprocal relationship where software delivery performance drives Lean product management practices, which in turn drive delivery performance — each improvement reinforces the other (Ch 8).

**Westrum organizational culture** — Typology classifying organizations as pathological (power-oriented), bureaucratic (rule-oriented), or generative (performance-oriented) based on information flow and cooperation patterns (Ch 3).

**WIP limits** — Work-in-process limits restricting the number of items in progress at once; surface bottlenecks and drive process improvement (Ch 7).
