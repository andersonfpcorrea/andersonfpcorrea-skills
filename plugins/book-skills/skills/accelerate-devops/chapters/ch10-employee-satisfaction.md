# Chapter 10: Employee Satisfaction, Identity, and Engagement

## Core Idea
Employee loyalty, organizational identity, and job satisfaction are measurable outcomes that both drive and are driven by DevOps practices; high performers are 2.2× more likely to recommend their org as a great place to work.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Employee Net Promoter Score (eNPS)**: Applied to employees using two questions:
  1. Would you recommend your ORGANIZATION as a place to work?
  2. Would you recommend your TEAM as a place to work?
  Scores 9–10 = promoters; 7–8 = passives; 0–6 = detractors. eNPS = % promoters − % detractors.

- **Organizational Identity Construct**: Six Likert-type items measuring the extent to which employees identify with their organization:
  - Glad they chose this org over another.
  - Talk about the org as a great place to work to friends.
  - Willing to put in extra effort to help the org succeed.
  - Values align with the organization's values.
  - Believe people work toward the same goal.
  - Feel the organization cares about them.

## Key Concepts
- **Organizational identity**: The degree to which employees see themselves as part of the organization's mission — a predictor of generative culture and organizational performance.
- **Values alignment**: When personal and org values align, burnout decreases. When they diverge, burnout increases — the "lived" values (not mission statements) are what matter.
- **Job satisfaction**: Measured as: satisfaction with work, having tools/resources to do the work, and the work making good use of skills and abilities. Automation that frees humans from rote tasks is a major driver.

## Mental Models
- Identity → engagement → performance → satisfaction → identity: a virtuous circle. CD and Lean practices contribute to identity, which contributes to performance, which reinforces satisfaction.
- "I hired them from you" (Adrian Cockcroft, Netflix): people who feel their best work is possible leave for environments where it is. Organizations that invest in their people retain talent.

## Key Takeaways
1. High performers: employees 2.2× more likely to recommend their organization, 1.8× more likely to recommend their team.
2. eNPS is correlated with customer feedback practices, work flow visibility, and employees' identification with organizational goals — not just HR practices.
3. Automation (test, deployment, monitoring) makes work more satisfying by enabling people to apply judgment rather than perform rote tasks.
4. Diversity matters for team performance and business outcomes; but diversity without inclusion doesn't take hold.
5. In 2017: 91% of respondents male, 6% female, 3% non-binary — 33% worked on teams with no women.
