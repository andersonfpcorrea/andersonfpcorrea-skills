# Chapter 3: Measuring and Changing Culture

## Core Idea
Culture can be measured quantitatively using Westrum's typology; generative culture predicts software delivery performance, and you can move toward it by changing behaviors — not by trying to change beliefs first.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Westrum Organizational Culture Typology**: Three types distinguished by how they handle information flow, cooperation, and failure.

| Dimension | Pathological (Power-Oriented) | Bureaucratic (Rule-Oriented) | Generative (Performance-Oriented) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cooperation | Low | Modest | High |
| Messengers | Shot | Neglected | Trained |
| Responsibility | Shirked | Narrow | Shared |
| Bridging | Discouraged | Tolerated | Encouraged |
| Failure | Leads to scapegoating | Leads to justice | Leads to inquiry |
| Novelty | Crushed | Leads to problems | Implemented |

- **Westrum as a Continuum**: The three types are points on a scale, measurable via Likert-type questions (1–7 agreement with statements about cooperation, safety to share bad news, etc.). Calculate the mean score across items to get an "organizational culture temperature."

- **Behavior-First Culture Change** (John Shook / Lean): "The way to change culture is not to first change how people think, but instead to start by changing how people behave — what they do." Implementing CD and Lean management practices drives culture change as a side effect.

## Key Concepts
- **Generative culture**: Performance-oriented; information flows freely, failures are treated as learning opportunities, novelty is welcomed. Predicts software delivery and organizational performance.
- **Information flow quality**: Three criteria — answers the receiver's questions, is timely, and is presented so it can be acted on. Culture determines whether organizations achieve this.
- **Construct validity**: Statistical property confirming survey questions actually measure what they claim; the Westrum construct passes discriminant validity, convergent validity, and reliability tests.

## Mental Models
- Think of culture as the "operating system" of the organization — it sets the rules for how information moves, failures are handled, and risks are shared. DevOps tools and practices are applications running on that OS.
- You can act your way to a better culture — start with practices, observe the cultural shift, not the reverse.

## Anti-patterns
- **Throat-to-choke accident investigations**: Identifying a single responsible person ignores that failures in complex adaptive systems are emergent, not attributable to one actor. Human error is the start of investigation, not the conclusion.
- **Pathological use of metrics**: In pathological/bureaucratic cultures, measurement becomes control; people hide information. "Whenever there is fear, you get the wrong numbers" (Deming).

## Key Takeaways
1. Westrum culture is measurable, valid, and reliably predicts software delivery and organizational performance.
2. Generative culture requires high cooperation, trust, and information flow — and treats failure as inquiry, not blame.
3. The fastest route to a better culture is changing practices (continuous delivery, Lean management) — culture follows behavior.
4. In 2016: 31% of respondents were pathological, 48% bureaucratic, 21% generative — substantial room for improvement.
