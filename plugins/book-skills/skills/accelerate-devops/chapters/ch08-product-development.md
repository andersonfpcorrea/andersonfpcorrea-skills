# Chapter 8: Product Development

## Core Idea
Four Lean product development capabilities — small batches, flow visibility, customer feedback, and team experimentation authority — form a virtuous cycle with delivery performance that drives organizational outcomes.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Four Lean Product Development Capabilities**:
  1. **Small batches**: Features sliced into pieces completable in < 1 week, including MVPs.
  2. **Flow visibility**: Teams understand and can see the flow of work from business to customer, including status of products and features.
  3. **Customer feedback**: Organizations actively seek customer feedback and incorporate it into product design.
  4. **Team experimentation**: Development teams can create and update specifications without requiring approval from outside the team.

- **The Virtuous Cycle**: Software delivery performance predicts Lean product management practices, which in turn predict organizational performance. Improving either side strengthens the other — a self-reinforcing loop.

## Key Concepts
- **Minimum viable product (MVP)**: A prototype with just enough features to enable validated learning about the product and business model.
- **Faux Agile**: Teams following Agile rituals (standups, sprints) while still doing months of upfront requirements, big-batch releases, and treating customer feedback as an afterthought.
- **Team experimentation authority**: Teams can change requirements and specifications in response to discoveries during development, without external approval. Correlated with profitability, productivity, and market share.

## Mental Models
- Small batches enable A/B testing and rapid validated learning — the experimental approach to product development is highly correlated with the technical practices of CD.
- "Virtuous cycle" thinking: don't try to fix product management or delivery in isolation — they reinforce each other. Improving either creates momentum in both.

## Anti-patterns
- **Requirements handed down to development**: Teams with no authority over specifications feel disconnected from customer outcomes and produce worse products. Authority to change specs mid-development is essential.
- **Customer feedback as afterthought**: Waiting until features are "done" to collect feedback wastes entire cycles on work that doesn't delight users.
- **Big-batch feature development**: Multi-month projects without releases prevent feedback loops; small features deliverable in < 1 week is the benchmark.

## Key Takeaways
1. Lean product practices predict organizational performance (profitability, market share) directly.
2. Software delivery performance and Lean product management form a virtuous cycle — improving one improves the other.
3. Team experimentation authority — the ability to change specs without external approval — is a predictor of org performance.
4. The experimental approach to product development (A/B testing, MVPs, rapid iteration) requires strong CD practices as its technical substrate.
