# Chapters 12–15: The Research (Science Behind the Book)

## Core Idea
The research uses inferential predictive analysis with validated psychometric constructs, making claims stronger than correlation — they establish that the capabilities in this book predict performance outcomes, not merely correlate with them.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Six Levels of Data Analysis** (Leek Framework, ascending complexity):
  1. Descriptive (census reports, summary stats)
  2. Exploratory (find relationships, detect patterns — correlation)
  3. **Inferential predictive** (theory-driven hypothesis testing — used throughout this book)
  4. Predictive (forecasting future events from historical data)
  5. Causal (requires randomized studies — gold standard)
  6. Mechanistic (calculate exact variable changes for exact outcomes — physical sciences)

  The Accelerate research is Type 3: inferential predictive, using theory-based hypotheses tested against survey data.

- **Latent Construct Methodology**:
  - A latent construct measures something that cannot be measured directly (e.g., organizational culture) by combining multiple measurable manifest variables (survey items).
  - Statistical tests confirm: discriminant validity (unrelated things are unrelated), convergent validity (related things are related), and reliability (internal consistency).
  - Combining multiple items makes results robust against individual bad data points or bad actors.

- **Why Surveys Over System Data**:
  1. Speed and breadth — cross-org, cross-country data collection in 4–6 weeks.
  2. System data is incomplete — it only sees inside system boundaries, not external behavior.
  3. Terminology ambiguity — "lead time" and "cycle time" mean different things to different teams; consistent survey wording standardizes definitions.
  4. Some things can only be measured through human perception (e.g., culture, job satisfaction).
  5. Survey data, properly designed, can be as trustworthy as system data.

## Key Concepts
- **Primary research**: Researchers collect new data (vs. secondary research which analyzes existing data). Accelerate is primary research via annual surveys.
- **Snowball sampling**: Survey participants refer others; appropriate when the population (DevOps practitioners) cannot be enumerated and is potentially averse to being studied.
- **Common method variance**: Statistical test checking that having the same respondents answer all questions hasn't artificially inflated relationships. Addressed in the research design.
- **Cross-sectional design**: Data collected at one point in time per year; enables year-over-year trend analysis but not longitudinal tracking of individuals.

## Mental Models
- "Correlation doesn't imply causation" — but inferential predictive analysis with theory-based hypotheses is significantly stronger than exploratory correlation. The relationships in this book are stated as predictions, not just associations.
- Latent constructs work like a Venn diagram: each question captures a related facet of the underlying concept; their combination is more reliable than any single measure.

## Key Takeaways
1. The claims in Part I are inferential predictive, not merely correlational — practices are tested as predictors of outcomes.
2. Survey data quality is rigorously validated; it is not inferior to system data and captures things system data cannot.
3. Latent constructs (like Westrum culture score, transformational leadership score) are validated via multiple statistical tests before being used in analyses.
4. The survey population is biased toward DevOps-familiar practitioners — organizations not using modern practices are likely performing even worse than the "low performers" in the data.
