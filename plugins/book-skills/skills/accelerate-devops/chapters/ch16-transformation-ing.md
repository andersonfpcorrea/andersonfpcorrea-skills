# Chapter 16: High-Performance Leadership and Management (ING Case Study)

## Core Idea
ING Netherlands demonstrates that a high-performance management framework requires developing a learning organization — you cannot implement culture change, only create the conditions for people to learn their way into it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Obeya (360-Degree Panorama Room)**: A physical/visual management room where the Tribe lead's work, priorities, and action items are made visible across four zones: strategic improvement, performance monitoring, portfolio roadmap, and leadership actions. Each IT objective ties directly to enterprise strategy.

- **Catchball**: The communication pattern connecting squad stand-ups to tribe leads and back. Problems escalate up through IT-area leads to tribe leads; solutions relay back down. Creates vertical and horizontal learning flow — front-line teams inform strategy; strategy informs team priorities.

- **ING Tribe/Squad/Chapter Structure**:
  - **Tribe**: Line-of-business unit delivering a portfolio of related products (e.g., Mortgage Services Tribe).
  - **Squad**: Self-steering team with a distinct customer mission, guided by a product owner, sized by Bezos' Two Pizza Rule.
  - **Chapter**: Cross-squad community of practitioners from the same discipline (e.g., Data Analytics Chapter) promoting specialized learning.
  - **BizDevOps**: ING's cross-functional composition — engineers and marketers on the same squad.

- **ING Transformation Principles**:
  1. Develop your own coaches — outside consultants can establish foundations, but you must own the change.
  2. Leaders change first — transformation starts with leader behavior, not delegation.
  3. Standard work enables learning — standard routines free cognitive energy for improvement.
  4. "Going deep before going wide" — sustained local learning before scaling changes.
  5. Don't copy behaviors, understand the why — implementation without comprehension produces fragile mimicry.

## Key Concepts
- **Learning organization**: An organization in which every team continuously learns, adapts, and shares learning with others — creating a natural flow of knowledge vertically and horizontally.
- **Hoshin Kanri (strategy deployment)**: Lean term for the alignment of front-line learning with strategic priorities; ING's catchball pattern is its implementation.
- **PDCA at all levels**: Plan-Do-Check-Act as a universal problem-solving rhythm for both squads and senior leaders.

## Mental Models
- "You can't implement culture change — culture is what emerges when you change the way people work and develop new habits and routines."
- The coaching team's discipline to "go deep before going wide" is the answer to the organizational temptation to scale before understanding is embedded.

## Key Takeaways
1. High-performance culture cannot be purchased from consultants or copied from another company — it must be developed through experimentation and learning adapted to your context.
2. Visual management (Obeya, squad boards) creates transparency that enables learning to flow through the organization.
3. Leader behavior must change first — demanding change from teams while maintaining old leader behavior is contradictory.
4. ING's result: "We give them speed with quality. Sometimes we take a little longer to reach green, but once we achieve it, we tend to stay green."
